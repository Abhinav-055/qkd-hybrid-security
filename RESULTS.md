## 2. Cost of hybridisation (1000 runs per configuration)

`results/benchmark.csv`, `results/benchmark_summary.json`. 8000 handshakes

| hybrid | ML-KEM-512 | 11.116 ms | 15.482 ms | 6.609 ms | 1757 B | 1650 B | 0.61 |
| pure PQC | ML-KEM-512 | 2.807 ms | 5.393 ms | 2.906 ms | 1721 B | 1650 B | 0.78 |
| hybrid | ML-KEM-768 | 14.439 ms | 18.137 ms | 8.969 ms | 2461 B | 2290 B | 1.04 |
| pure PQC | ML-KEM-768 | 4.477 ms | 6.996 ms | 4.625 ms | 2425 B | 2290 B | 1.20 |

| pure PQC | ML-KEM-1024 | 6.387 ms | 9.896 ms | 6.703 ms | 3291 B | 3250 B | 1.04 |

### The delta

| KEM | latency delta | overhead | CPU delta | wire delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | **+8.309 ms** | +296.0% | +3.703 ms | **+36 B** |
| ML-KEM-768 | **+9.963 ms** | +222.5% | +4.344 ms | **+36 B** |
| ML-KEM-1024 | **+10.969 ms** | +171.7% | +5.406 ms | **+36 B** |
| FrodoKEM-976-AES | **+5.876 ms** | +9.2% | +3.172 ms | **+36 B** |

Three things to read here:

* **The cost is roughly constant in absolute terms** (+5.9 to +11.0 ms), so
  the percentage is an artefact of how fast the baseline is. It is dominated
  by the two HTTP round trips to the KME — `enc_keys` for the initiator,
  `dec_keys` for the responder — not by cryptography. That is why hybridising
  ML-KEM-512 looks catastrophic (+296%) and hybridising FrodoKEM looks nearly
  free (+9.2%): the KEM got slower, the QKD fetch did not.
* **The wire delta is a flat +36 bytes** at every security level — the
  36-character QKD `key_ID` in M1. Key material never travels on the
  handshake wire, which is the point of the ETSI `dec_keys` design.
* **Wire cost is dominated by the KEM, by two orders of magnitude.**
  FrodoKEM-976-AES costs 63 KB per handshake against ML-KEM-768's 4.75 KB, for
  the same NIST level 3. If bytes matter, that choice matters far more than
  hybridisation does.

For reference, the KEM parameters in play:

| KEM | NIST level | public key | ciphertext | shared secret |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-512 | 1 | 800 B | 768 B | 32 B |
| ML-KEM-768 | 3 | 1184 B | 1088 B | 32 B |
| ML-KEM-1024 | 5 | 1568 B | 1568 B | 32 B |
| FrodoKEM-976-AES | 3 | 15632 B | 15792 B | 24 B |

## 4. Headline experiment: the silent downgrade

`results/downgrade_events.csv` (9,900 handshakes), summarised in
`results/downgrade_summary.csv`. Fixed 10 kbps supply, `REFERENCE_SILENT`,
9 load levels × 30 s.

| offered | total hps | hybrid hps | downgrade rate | **reported to app** | P95 latency | QKD utilisation | mean pool |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.033 | **0.000** | 19.8 ms | 0.025 | 526.0 |
| 5 | 5.00 | 4.97 | 0.007 | **0.000** | 18.5 ms | 0.127 | 463.5 |
| 10 | 10.00 | 9.97 | 0.003 | **0.000** | 20.6 ms | 0.255 | 398.0 |
| 20 | 20.00 | 19.96 | 0.002 | **0.000** | 22.9 ms | 0.511 | 284.5 |
| 30 | 30.00 | 29.90 | 0.003 | **0.000** | 20.1 ms | 0.765 | 140.9 |
| **39** | 39.00 | 38.93 | 0.002 | **0.000** | 20.4 ms | 0.997 | 18.4 |
| 50 | 50.00 | **39.06** | 0.219 | **0.000** | 20.0 ms | 1.000 | 17.1 |
| 75 | 74.99 | **38.96** | 0.480 | **0.000** | 20.8 ms | 0.997 | 14.7 |
| 100 | 99.48 | **38.98** | **0.608** | **0.000** | 23.4 ms | 0.998 | 3.8 |

* **Peak measured hybrid throughput: 39.06 hps = 1.00× the theoretical
  `supply/256`.** Not approximately — the same figure to four significant
  digits.
* **Knee at 39 hps, first degradation at 50 hps**, bracketing 39.06 from both
  sides. Detected numerically by `detect_knee()`, not by eye.
* **QKD utilisation rises linearly to 0.997 at the knee and pins at ~1.000
  above it.** The supply is exactly, fully consumed from the knee onward;
  nothing is being wasted, there simply isn't more.
* **`reported to app` is 0.000 at every single load level**, including the one
  where 60.8% of sessions lost their QKD contribution.
* **P95 latency is flat at ~20 ms throughout** — 20.4 ms at the knee, 23.4 ms
  at 2.5× over it.

That last pair of rows is the finding. The degradation is simultaneously
invisible in throughput, invisible in latency, and invisible in the
handshake's return value. Every session key is well-formed, agreed by both
parties and MAC-confirmed. A throughput dashboard, an SLO monitor and an
integration suite would all report a perfectly healthy service. The only
place the truth appears is a `security_mode` field that nothing is obliged to
read.

### 5.1 Admission control — silent failure becomes loud failure

Silent downgrades hit exactly **0.000** at every load level, and it is
**nearly free below the knee**: at 39 hps it rejects 0.1% of offers and still
delivers the full 39.0 hybrid hps at 0.997 utilisation. Above the knee it
pins throughput at the ceiling and refuses the remainder — 34.5% at 60 hps,
60.7% at 100 hps.

Note it holds *both* properties simultaneously: no wasted keys
(utilisation ~1.0) and no degraded sessions. What it gives up is sessions.
Use it when a non-hybrid session is worse than no session; do not use it when
a rejected connection is the greater harm.

Figure: `admission_control_comparison.png`.

### 5.2 Per-peer quota — fairness that destroys key material

The counter-intuitive result. At 39 hps — exactly at the ceiling — the quota
delivers **26.5** hybrid hps where the unmitigated reference delivers
**38.9**. Enforcing fairness destroyed **32%** of the available
information-theoretic security. QKD utilisation confirms it independently:
**0.679**, so 32% of the supply went unconsumed.

The cause is that a fixed quota is **not work-conserving**. Each peer is
allowed `39.06 / 4 = 9.77` keys/s. With demand split 4:1:1:1 the greedy peer
wants 22.29 and is capped at 9.77, while each light peer wants 5.57 against a
9.77 allowance — so 12.58 keys/s sits unused and *cannot be lent*. Predicted
throughput is `Σ min(demand_p, quota_p)`:

| offered | predicted | measured | QKD util |
|---|---|---|---|
| 39 hps | 26.48 | **26.5** | 0.679 |
| 60 hps | 35.48 | **35.4** | 0.906 |
| 100 hps | 39.06 | **38.8** | 0.994 |

Three load levels, agreement to three significant figures, confirmed by a
second independent metric. The loss equals the stranded allowance,
`Σ max(0, quota_p − demand_p)`, and vanishes once every peer's demand exceeds
its quota — which is why it is invisible at 100 hps and why a short pilot
sampling only 10 and 100 hps reported the quota as free.

What it does buy is real: Jain's index 0.645 → 1.000 and the greedy peer's
capture of hybrid sessions 0.572 → 0.248, against a fair share of 0.250.

**Recommendation: use a work-conserving variant** — a borrowing or
hierarchical token bucket, or weighted fair queueing over the pool rather
than fixed partitioning. This is the clearest future work the data points to.
See `DECISIONS.md` D18.

Figure: `quota_comparison.png`.

### 5.3 Explicit downgrade signal — free, and fixes nothing

Throughput, hybrid throughput and utilisation are identical to the reference
at every load level. Silent downgrades go to 0.000 and *signalled* downgrades
take their place at exactly the same rate (0.610 at 100 hps). It prevents no
downgrade; it converts an unknown-unknown into a known risk the caller can
act on.

Since it costs nothing measurable, there is no reason not to run it. It is
the single most important operational recommendation in the study.

Figure: `downgrade_signal_comparison.png`.

## 6. Security analysis

`analysis/security_matrix.md`, machine-readable in
`results/security_matrix.csv`.

| | KEM intact | KEM broken |
|---|---|---|
| **QKD intact** | **A** — full hybrid security; the adversary must break both | **B** — confidentiality survives (information-theoretic QKD contribution); designed authentication lost, residual only from the QKD key as an implicit PSK |
| **QKD absent/broken** | **C** — computational security only; nothing fails today, but the hedge is gone. **The cell this study populates** | **D** — no security; the deterministic KDF yields the session key |

`analysis/game_hops.md` traces which hop discharges the claim in each cell:
G2–G4 (KEM IND-CCA) for Cell C, G5 (QKD uniformity) for Cell B, either for
Cell A, neither for Cell D. Hop G5 becomes **vacuous** when the pool is
empty — the silent downgrade, stated formally.

## Measurement conditions

* Single host: Windows 11, Python 3.14.2, liboqs 0.16.0 via liboqs-python
  0.16.0. Client, server and both handshake roles share the machine, so
  absolute latencies include no network path.
* **The host slows under sustained load**, up to 1.6× within a single 1000-run
  configuration. Consequences: the benchmark interleaves its two protocols so
  the paired delta survives drift (`DECISIONS.md` D17), each configuration
  reports `latency_drift_ratio`, and **absolute latencies should be read as
  indicative while deltas, ratios and wire sizes are trustworthy**.
* Per-run `cpu_ms` is quantised by the OS scheduler tick (15.625 ms); use
  `cpu_ms_mean`, which is exact (`DECISIONS.md` D16).
* The load generator runs 16 workers, which sustain ~112 hybrid handshakes/s
  against a 100 hps top offered rate (`DECISIONS.md` D14). Offers it could
  not attempt are counted in `generator_saturated`: **0 across the entire
  sweep and mitigation study**, and 14 of 9,900 (0.14%) in the 100 hps
  downgrade row.
* Pools are drained to a defined empty state before and after warm-up
  (`DECISIONS.md` D12), which is why below-ceiling rows show ~0.002–0.033
  rather than exactly 0.
* The mock KME models key *supply* only, never QKD physics. `ε_QKD` is a
  black-box parameter throughout.
